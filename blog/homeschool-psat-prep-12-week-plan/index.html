Homeschool PSAT Prep: A 12-Week Study Plan

How to prep for the PSAT at home: what the digital PSAT/NMSQT covers, how a homeschool student registers, and what to do across twelve weeks.

November 10, 2026

Homeschool PSAT prep does not require a class. It requires a clear picture of the digital test, a seat at a school that gives it, and a PSAT study plan aimed at skills that are actually weak. This is that plan: twelve weeks at home for the PSAT/NMSQT.

The PSAT 10 and the PSAT 8/9 cover the same skills for younger grades. Only the PSAT/NMSQT, in the right year of high school, qualifies for National Merit.

What the digital test is like

Most students take the PSAT/NMSQT in College Board's Bluebook app, on a laptop or tablet, not a phone. Reading and Writing comes first, then Math, with a short break between them. Each section has two modules. You can move around inside a module. Once you leave a module, you cannot go back.

The second module is harder or easier based on the first, and both use the same score scale. Some accommodated testing is nonadaptive and a bit longer. Use that approved timing.

College Board's student guide gives these figures for the adaptive test:

SectionQuestionsTime
Reading and Writing5464 minutes, two modules of 32
Math4470 minutes, two modules of 35

Testing is about 2 hours and 14 minutes, plus check-in and the break. Four Reading and Writing questions and four Math questions are unscored pretest items, mixed in so you cannot spot them. Treat every question as real.

Section scores run from 160 to 760. The total, 320 to 1520, is their sum. National Merit uses a Selection Index instead: double Reading and Writing, add Math, divide by 10. The index runs from 48 to 228. College Board's example is Reading and Writing 620 plus Math 500, which is a Selection Index of 174. Cutoffs change by year, and Semifinalist cutoffs also change by state.

Reading and Writing is one question per short passage, from literature, history and social studies, the humanities, or science. Similar skills are grouped from easier to harder. College Board's ranges for the section:

  • Craft and Structure, 13 to 15: words in context, structure, and two related texts.
  • Information and Ideas, 12 to 14: main ideas, evidence, inferences.
  • Standard English Conventions, 11 to 15: boundaries, form, punctuation.
  • Expression of Ideas, 8 to 12: transitions and combining notes into a sentence.

Math ranges for the same adaptive test:

  • Algebra, 13 to 15: linear equations, functions, systems, inequalities.
  • Advanced Math, 12 to 14: quadratics, exponentials, other nonlinear work.
  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, 7 to 9: ratios, percents, data, probability.
  • Geometry and Trigonometry, 4 to 6: area, volume, lines, triangles, right-triangle trigonometry.

About 30 percent of math items are set in a real situation. Some are multiple choice. Some ask for a typed answer. Bluebook has a formula sheet and a Desmos calculator, scientific or graphing, for all of Math. A student may bring an approved calculator too. There is no no-calculator block.

Scores weigh right and wrong answers, question difficulty, and how likely a guess was. For most students, College Board says, answering is better than leaving a blank, especially after crossing out a choice or two.

How a homeschool student registers

The PSAT/NMSQT is arranged by a school. Homeschooled students are away students: contact a school that gives the test and ask to be added to its registration file at least four weeks before the window. Some districts need longer, and some states do not allow away students, so confirm that first.

Bring a legal name, a date of birth, and the fact of homeschooling. Add the Services for Students with Disabilities number if accommodations are already approved. Ask who provides the device. An away student needs a photo ID on test day.

In the 2026 guide the window is October 1 through October 30 and the fee is $18, though a school may add an administrative charge. A student gets one valid score in the window. Later years publish their own dates and fee. Count backward from the school's date.

On a personal device, install Bluebook early, check the approved-device list, and plan on about three hours of battery. Personal Chromebooks are not allowed. Bring a pen or pencil. Keep the laptop open until staff confirm the submission. Closing the lid can lose the answers.

A usual four-year student must take the qualifying PSAT/NMSQT in 11th grade. A 10th-grade score does not enter National Merit. Early graduation, a five-year plan, and dual enrollment have their own rules in the student guide. Read them, or ask National Merit Scholarship Corporation, before that fall. Recognition for a homeschooled student is mailed to the address given at exam setup. Request accommodations through the testing school at least seven weeks ahead.

Before week 1

A 10th grader is learning the format. An 11th grader on a four-year path is also producing the National Merit score, so save one official practice test for the last week.

Run the free PSAT placement check before you pick topics. It takes about fifteen to twenty minutes in any AI chat. It asks which PSAT this is, whether National Merit is in play, and where Reading and Writing and Math are thin, then names a week to start.

Ask the testing school for a Bluebook practice sign-in. Use official questions when you want a result you can trust. An AI assistant can explain a miss or drill one skill, but it sometimes writes an item with no right answer, or with two. Tell it to solve the question before it writes the choices.

The twelve weeks

For an early October date, week 1 is mid-July. One session a week is enough: about an hour for the first eight, longer once the sections are timed. Keep an error log, and start each week by reviewing last week's misses.

Week 1. Run the placement check and keep two or three skills, not the whole outline. Algebra and evidence questions repay the time. Write the list down.

Week 2. Learn the modules, the timer, mark-for-review, the formula sheet, and the rule that a finished module stays finished. Try Desmos once. Confirm from the current guide whether this sitting counts for National Merit.

Week 3. Command of evidence: one passage, one question, and the student has to point at the support. Then a short timed set. Reading and Writing pace is a little over a minute a question.

Week 4. Boundaries, form, punctuation, and transitions. Name why the wrong choice is wrong, and review every miss before you stop.

Week 5. Linear equations, functions, systems, and inequalities, with scratch work on paper. The formula sheet does not choose the formula.

Week 6. Whatever week 1 flagged next: often nonlinear equations, sometimes ratios and data, sometimes the short geometry set. Skip skills the check already called solid.

Week 7. Pacing. You cannot borrow time from the next module. Mark a stall, move, come back, and answer every question before the module ends.

Week 8. The PSAT/NMSQT and the SAT share a score scale, so a section score means the same level of work on either test. Note which week 1 skills are still open. Those are the only ones you reopen.

For each week already scripted, with the tutor instructions included, use the PSAT Prep Study Plan, 12-Week AI Tutor Guide. You still run it at home. It is not a College Board or National Merit product, and it promises no score.

Week 9. Both Reading and Writing modules, timed, with a same-day review. The harder items sit at the end of each skill group.

Week 10. Both Math modules, with the test-day calculator. Review typed answers as carefully as the multiple choice. Pace is about a minute and a half a question.

Week 11. Both sections in order, with the break and no hints. The error log should be shorter than week 1. That list is the last week.

Week 12. Dress rehearsal. Use a leftover official Bluebook practice test if you have one: charged device, quiet room, a pen, no phone. If you do not, run your best timed sets and treat the number as practice. Confirm the time, the device, and the photo ID, then stop.

After the score

Read the score range and, on the PSAT/NMSQT, the Selection Index. Check the entry-requirements answers on the report. A wrong one can keep a real score out of National Merit, and there is a deadline to correct it. File the error log if this was a practice year. In the qualifying year, take the same approach to the SAT.

Timings, the Selection Index, the 2026 window and fee, and the away-student rules above come from College Board's PSAT/NMSQT Student Guide, its score guide for families, and its page for homeschooled and away students. If a later guide changes a date or a fee, follow the later guide.
