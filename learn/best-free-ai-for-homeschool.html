ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot: Which Free AI Works Best for Homeschool?

A practical comparison for homeschool families: what matters when choosing an AI chat assistant to tutor with, and why the prompt matters more than the brand.

2026-11-02

Families often spend more time choosing an AI assistant than using one. Here is the short version: the brand matters less than you think, and the first message matters more.

What actually matters

When you pick an assistant to tutor with, check four things:

  1. It follows instructions. Does it keep your rules when your child pushes for the answer? This is the most important test.
  2. You can use it. Is there an app or browser version your family already knows?
  3. Age rules. Each service sets its own minimum age, and some require 18. If your child is under that age, the adult holds the account and runs the session.
  4. Privacy settings. Can you turn off chat history or training on your conversations?

How the four compare for tutoring

  • ChatGPT is the most familiar. The free version handles weekly sessions well.
  • Claude tends to follow long instructions closely and writes clearly.
  • Gemini fits easily into a family already using Google accounts.
  • Copilot is built into Windows and Edge, which makes it easy to reach on a family PC.

All four have free versions that are enough for a weekly session. Features and limits change often, so check the current details before you rely on any one of them.

Why the prompt beats the brand

We test our guides on several AI models, including weaker open models, because a prompt that works on a weak model works better on a strong one. The biggest difference we see is not between brands. It is between a session with tutor rules at the top and one without. With rules, every assistant asks before it tells. Without them, every assistant hands over answers.

A simple way to decide

Run the same free placement check in two assistants and compare. Notice which one your child prefers and which one keeps its rules better. That's your tutor.

Questions parents ask

Which AI is best for tutoring kids?

The one your family can use comfortably and that follows instructions well. In our testing, the tutor rules matter far more than the brand. Any of the major assistants can run a good session when it is set up properly.

Is the free version good enough?

For a one-hour weekly tutoring session, yes. Free versions have usage limits and sometimes older models, but a focused session rarely hits them.

Can I switch assistants partway through a course?

Yes. Each session starts from a fresh prompt, so you can use ChatGPT one week and Gemini the next without losing anything.
