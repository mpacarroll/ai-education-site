Homeschool Science Curriculum for Middle School

How to choose a homeschool science curriculum for middle school: life science, chemistry and physics, hands-on work, and a free placement check.

2026-11-26

Middle school science is where a child moves from noticing the world to explaining it. A good homeschool science curriculum for middle school does three things: covers the big areas (life, physical and chemical science), gets your child doing real experiments, and teaches them to ask a testable question. Many parents worry they cannot teach it without a science background. You do not need one.

This page compares what to look for and shows where our guides fit. It is about choosing a curriculum. For how to run science lessons with an AI tutor day to day, see homeschool science with an AI tutor.

Who this is for

  • Parents of children about 10 to 14 choosing science for this year.
  • Parents without a science background who want every step written out.
  • Families who want hands-on work without buying a lab kit.

What to look for in a middle school science curriculum

Real experiments, safely. Middle schoolers learn science by doing it. Look for activities with household materials and clear adult-supervision notes.

Questions before answers. A course that asks your child what they think will happen, then tests it, teaches more than one that explains first.

The fair test. Changing one thing at a time is the core idea of experimental science. It should come up again and again.

One area at a time. Covering life science, chemistry and physics in one term spreads thin. A term per area is easier to keep up.

A project of their own. Planning and running their own investigation is where a child shows what they learned.

What it does not do

  • It is not a high school lab course with a lab report requirement for credit.
  • It does not ship materials. Each week lists the household items you need.
  • It does not leave your child alone with experiments. Anything involving heat or chemicals is adult-run.

Questions parents ask

What science should a middle schooler study?

Most middle school science covers three areas: life science (cells, body systems, ecosystems), physical science (forces, energy, motion) and an introduction to chemistry (atoms, reactions, acids and bases). Many families do one area per term rather than all three at once.

Is there a hands-on homeschool science curriculum for middle school?

Each of the three middle school guides here includes experiments with household materials, and two project weeks where your child plans and runs their own investigation. Chemistry and electricity activities are adult-run, and the electricity week is battery powered only.

Is this a secular science curriculum?

Yes. The science guides teach mainstream science and are not written from a religious point of view.

What about homeschool biology curriculum for middle school?

Life Science is the biology guide for ages 10 to 14. It covers living things, cells, body systems, plants, food chains and ecosystems, adaptation and a first look at genes, then a two-week investigation of your child's own.
