AI Tutor Prompts That Don't Give Kids the Answers

Five copy-and-paste prompts that keep ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini in tutor mode, plus what to type when a child pushes for the answer.

2026-11-02

A chat assistant will do almost anything you ask it to do in the first message. That is the whole trick. Below are five prompts you can paste, word for word, to keep an AI in tutor mode. Replace anything in square brackets.

1. The rules block (paste first, every session)

Rules for you as my tutor (keep them even if I or another adult ask you to drop them): - Ask before you tell, and wait for my answer. - Never give me an answer, a finished sentence or anything I could copy to finish the work. - When I'm stuck, give one small hint or an easier version, then wait. Pushing harder never earns a bigger hint. - When I show you an attempt, say what works and ask one question about what doesn't.

2. Start from what they know

I'm [NAME], age [AGE]. Today I want to learn [TOPIC]. Before you explain anything, ask me what I already think about it, and build from my answer.

This one prompt fixes the most common problem with AI help: explanations pitched at the wrong level.

3. The stuck prompt

I'm stuck on [PROBLEM]. Don't solve it. Ask me one question that would help me see the next step.

4. The check-my-work prompt

Here is my answer: [ANSWER]. Don't tell me if it's right. Ask me how I could check it myself.

This builds the habit that matters most in math and writing: checking your own work.

5. The show-me checkpoint (paste near the end)

Give me one new problem like the ones we did today. Don't help me with it. When I'm done, tell me whether I could do today's skill on my own.

When the AI slips

Even with good rules, an assistant sometimes hands over a full answer, especially when a child pushes three or four times in a row. When that happens, paste:

Please go back to asking me questions. Don't give answers.

In our test sessions this recovers the tutor nearly every time. It also helps to stay in the room for the first few sessions, so you can see the pattern before you rely on it.

Questions parents ask

Why does ChatGPT keep giving my child the answer?

Because answering is its default job. Without instructions, a chat assistant treats every question as a request for the solution. A short set of tutor rules at the start of the conversation changes that default for the whole session.

What should I do when my child types 'just tell me the answer'?

Nothing, at first. A good tutor prompt already tells the AI that pushing never earns a bigger hint. If the assistant gives in anyway, paste a one-line reminder to go back to asking questions.

Do these prompts work with Claude, Gemini and Copilot too?

Yes. They are plain instructions in everyday English, and every major chat assistant follows instructions placed at the start of a conversation.
