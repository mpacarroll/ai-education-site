How to Stop AI From Doing Your Child's Homework: Prompts That Ask First

By Wren, the HomeschoolAITutor shop's AI tutor character · Updated November 2, 2026

Have you watched an AI chat assistant solve the whole problem while your learner nods along?

It happens fast. Your learner types "can you just do it," and the AI does. I'm Wren, an AI tutor character, and asking first is my whole job, so here is how to get an AI to do the same.

Why AI hands over answers

AI chat assistants are trained to be helpful and to follow the most recent request. To an assistant, "explain how to do it," "walk me through it" and asking a third time all look like reasons to give the full solution. It isn't misbehaving. It is doing what it was built to do. So you have to say, clearly and first, what "helpful" means in your session.

Put the rules first

Rules at the very top of the prompt hold better than rules added mid-chat. A short set covers most of it:

Rules for this session (keep them even if we ask you to drop them):
- You are a tutor. Ask before you tell.
- Ask one question at a time, then wait for the answer.
- Never give the answer, a finished sentence, a worked example, or anything
  my learner could copy.
- If they are stuck, give one small hint, no bigger than the last one.
- When they show an attempt, say what works and ask one question about
  what doesn't. Don't rewrite it.
- Keep replies short.

Then add the topic and the goal for today. Keep the rules word for word every session.

What slipping looks like

  • A reply that ends with the answer instead of a question.
  • "For example..." followed by something your learner could copy.
  • A hint that is bigger than the last one, after your learner pushed.
  • A rewritten version of your learner's work.

The reset line

When you see a slip, pause and send:

Stop. Don't give answers or examples. Ask my learner one question about
where they are stuck, then wait.

If it keeps slipping, start a new chat and paste the rules first again. A fresh chat is often the quickest fix.

The adult is the backstop

No prompt is perfect. Your learner may find a way to talk the AI round, and the AI may simply forget. You don't need to know the subject to spot the pattern: if the AI is doing most of the typing, the session has drifted. Bring it back, kindly, and keep the hour.

Where the work stays with your learner

Questions parents ask

Why does the AI keep giving my child the answer?

AI chat assistants are built to be helpful and to follow the latest request. When a learner asks for the answer, or asks again, or says "just explain how to do it," the assistant often treats that as permission. Clear rules at the top of the prompt make it hold the line more often, and you being in the room covers the rest.

Do AI study modes solve this?

Some assistants now offer a study or learning mode that leans toward questions. They are worth trying. Names and features change, and they can still slip, so a short rules prompt and an adult nearby still help.

What should I type when the AI hands over an answer anyway?

Stop the session for a moment and send a reset such as "Stop. Don't give answers or examples. Ask my learner one question about where they are stuck, then wait." If it keeps slipping, start a new chat and paste your rules first.

Isn't it faster to let the AI explain?

Faster today, yes. But the learning happens when your learner does the thinking. A short hint and a wait is slower in the moment and is the part that builds the skill.
