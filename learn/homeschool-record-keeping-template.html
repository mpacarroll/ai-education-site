Homeschool Record Keeping Template, Printable

A printable homeschool record keeping template: attendance, hours, subjects, books read and a weekly plan, plus what to keep and how often.

2026-11-26

A homeschool record keeping template does two jobs. It holds whatever your state or region asks you to keep, and it holds your own memory of what this year actually covered. The second one matters more than most families expect when it is time to plan next year or write a summary.

This page is for parents looking for a printable record system. It covers what to keep, how often, and the printable kits we sell on Etsy.

Who this is for

  • Parents in their first year who want a simple system from day one.
  • Families who need attendance or hours for an annual review.
  • Parents who keep records on scraps of paper and want one binder.

First, check your own requirements

Record rules vary a great deal by state and country, and they change. Read your own jurisdiction's current rules directly before you decide what to keep. This page is about records that are useful to you, not about meeting any particular legal requirement.

What a homeschool record keeping template should hold

Attendance or hours. A day or an hour count, filled in as you go.

Subjects covered. A line per subject per week is enough to show the shape of the year.

Books read. A running list, with dates, is one of the most useful records you can keep.

Work samples. A few dated pieces per subject, kept in a folder.

A weekly plan. Planning and recording on the same page saves a step.

A year summary. One page at the end, written from the weekly records.

Keep it light. A record system that takes ten minutes a day gets abandoned by October. One that takes two minutes after each lesson and ten minutes on Friday tends to last the year. Store finished pages in one binder, by month, and keep a scanned copy if your state asks for records to be kept for several years.

Questions parents ask

What records should a homeschool keep?

It depends on where you live, so check your own state or region's current rules first. Most families find it useful to keep attendance or hours, subjects covered, books read, samples of work and a short summary at the end of the year.

Is there a free homeschool record keeping template?

A plain spreadsheet with a row per day and a column per subject works, and many families start there. A printable kit saves you from designing the pages and keeps the year in one binder.

Is a printable or an app better for homeschool records?

Either works if you use it every week. Paper is easy to file and show. An app is easy to search. Whatever you choose, keep the records in files you own, not only inside one service.

How often should I update homeschool records?

A few minutes at the end of each day or each week is enough if you write things down as they happen. A short Friday review keeps the year from piling up.
