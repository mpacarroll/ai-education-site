Homeschool Writing Curriculum for Middle School

How to pick a homeschool writing curriculum for middle school, from voice and paragraphs to persuasion and editing, plus a free writing placement check.

2026-11-26

Middle school is when writing stops being about neat sentences and starts being about thinking on paper: making a point, backing it up, and explaining something so a reader follows it. It is also when many children decide they hate writing. A good homeschool writing curriculum for middle school has to handle both.

This page is for parents of children about 10 to 14 who are choosing a writing course. It covers what the course should teach, how to check your child's level first, and where our guide fits.

Who this is for

  • Parents of a middle schooler who writes very little, or writes a lot without a clear point.
  • Parents who are not confident about their own writing and want the teaching scripted.
  • Families who want writing to happen weekly without a fight.

Check your child's level first

Writing levels vary widely in the middle school years. Some children need sentence and paragraph work; others are ready for persuasion and evidence. What to look for in a writing curriculum

It starts with voice. A child who believes their own words are worth writing down will practice. A child who thinks writing means sounding like a worksheet will not.

It builds the paragraph before the essay. A paragraph that states a point and keeps it is the unit every longer piece is made of.

It covers more than one kind of writing. Story, explanation and persuasion use different skills. Middle school is the time to practice all three.

It treats editing as a skill. Revising for a reader is where most of the learning happens. A curriculum that ends at the first draft skips it.

It ends with something real. A piece your child chose, planned and polished teaches more than another prompt from a list.

Where our guide fits

Write Like You Mean It is a 12-week plan for ages 10 to 16. It is a PDF you run with any AI chat assistant, and the free versions of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot all work. Each week is one scripted session of about 60 minutes, with every step written out for you.

The weeks go: voice first, sentences that work, the paragraph promise, show don't tell, story shapes, two weeks on persuasion (knowing your reader, then evidence), explaining clearly, and editing. Weeks 10 to 12 are a project your child picks, plans, drafts, polishes and publishes.

The tutor rules in every prompt tell the AI to coach and never to write for your child. It asks about the draft and points at what to fix. The words stay your child's.

What it does not do

  • It is not a grammar and spelling course. If mechanics are the main gap, Grammar and Spelling That Stick covers them for ages 8 to 12.
  • It does not grade essays or produce a transcript.
  • It does not let the AI write. If your child asks the AI to just write it, the prompts tell it to decline and ask a question instead.

Questions parents ask

What should a middle school writing curriculum cover?

Clear sentences, paragraphs that make and keep a point, narrative, explanation, and persuasion with evidence, plus editing as a skill of its own. By the end a child should be able to plan, draft and revise a piece for a real reader.

How much writing should a middle schooler do each week?

Regular short pieces teach more than one long essay a month. Write Like You Mean It is one guided session of about an hour a week, and most weeks end with a short piece of writing your child revises.

My child hates writing. Where do I start?

Start with voice. Children who hate writing often believe they have nothing to say or that it has to sound like a textbook. Week 1 of the guide is about writing the way they talk, before any rules about sentences or paragraphs.

Can AI help with writing without writing it for my child?

Yes, if it is set up as an editor and not a ghostwriter. The tutor rules in every prompt tell the AI to ask questions about the draft and point at what to fix, never to rewrite it. You protect that rule in the room.
