SAT Prep for Homeschool Students: A Plan

SAT prep for homeschool students: how to plan the year, the PSAT and ACT, a 12-week study plan run with any AI, and a free placement check.

2026-11-26

SAT prep for a homeschool student is mostly the same as for anyone else: find the gaps, drill them, then practice under time. What differs is the planning. There is no school counselor setting the calendar, so your family chooses when to take the PSAT, when to sit the SAT, and whether to try the ACT instead.

This page is for parents of teens about 14 to 18 planning test prep at home. It covers how to plan the year and where our three study plans fit.

Who this is for

  • Parents of a homeschooled teen taking the SAT for the first time.
  • Families deciding between the SAT and the ACT.
  • Teens who want a structured plan they can run at home, without a live class.

Run the free SAT placement check. It is a 15-minute chat with any AI assistant that asks one question at a time, gives no hints, and sorts each skill into solid, shaky or missing. There are matching checks for the PSAT and the ACT, and the placement checks page lists every subject.

How to plan the year

Pick a test date first. Work backward from it. Twelve weeks of prep fits before most dates if you start early.

Leave room for a retake. Plan the first sitting early enough that a second try still fits before any deadline that matters.

Decide on the PSAT. It is a lower-stakes practice of the same skills. A sophomore or junior can use it as a dry run.

Try both tests if unsure. A timed practice SAT and a timed practice ACT, a week apart, usually make the choice clear.

Mix in real practice tests. Use official practice tests for timed runs, and keep a log of every miss and why it happened. The log decides what to study next.

Check the official rules yourself. Dates, registration and accommodations are set by the testing organizations. Use the College Board's SAT site for the SAT, and the testing organization's own site for the ACT.

Questions parents ask

Do homeschoolers do better on SAT?

We know of no source we can point to that settles it. A score depends on preparation, not on where a student went to school. What a homeschooled teen often has is more control over when to study and when to sit the test.

How can my homeschooler take the SAT?

The College Board sets the dates, registration steps and test centers, and they can change from year to year. Check its SAT site for the current steps before you plan around a date.

Is SAT or ACT better for ADHD?

Neither test is better for every student with ADHD. The two differ in pacing and in how sections are laid out, so a timed practice test of each usually shows which one suits your teen. Testing accommodations are requested through each testing organization, so check their sites for the current process.

Should my homeschooler take the PSAT, the SAT or the ACT?

The PSAT is a practice run and, for some juniors, a scholarship qualifier. Between the SAT and the ACT, many students try a practice test of each and pick the one that suits them. Our three plans start with the same kind of check, so the comparison is fair.

How long should SAT prep take?

Our plan runs 12 weeks at one session a week, plus practice between sessions. Starting early enough to retake the test once is a sound plan.
