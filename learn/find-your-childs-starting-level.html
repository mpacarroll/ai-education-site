How to Find Your Child's Starting Level Before Buying Curriculum

Why placement matters more than which curriculum you pick, and how a free 15-minute AI placement check names the right week to start.

2026-11-02

Most curriculum mistakes are placement mistakes. A learner who starts too low is bored by week two. A learner who starts too high stops trying by week three. Both look like "this curriculum doesn't work," and neither one is the curriculum's fault.

Why grade level isn't enough

Grade level tells you where a child is supposed to be. It does not tell you that your nine-year-old reads like a twelve-year-old but freezes at fractions, or that your teenager can write well but never learned to plan an essay. Real learners are uneven, and homeschooling is the one setting where you can teach to the unevenness.

What a good placement check does

A placement check is not a test with a grade. It does three things:

  1. Asks without teaching. Each question is asked once, with no hints, so you see what your learner can do alone today.
  2. Covers each skill area briefly. Five or so short areas, a few questions each, instead of one long worksheet.
  3. Ends with a decision. Not just a score, but which week to start at and the one or two gaps that matter most for the first month.

How ours work

Every one of our courses has a free placement check. You paste one prompt into any AI chat assistant: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot, free versions included. The AI asks your learner one question at a time and says only "Thanks, got it" in between, so it never gives away answers. After about 15 minutes it writes a short report: each question, what your learner answered, a score for each area, and the week to start.

Save the report. If you go on to the course, you paste it under that week's launch prompt, and the AI tutor picks up from what the check found.

When to run one

  • Before you buy anything. Fifteen minutes now can save a semester on the wrong level.
  • At a natural break. Winter break and the start of a new term are good moments to recheck.
  • When something feels off. If a learner suddenly struggles, a check often shows a gap from two levels back.
