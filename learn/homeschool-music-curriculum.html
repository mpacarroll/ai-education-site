Homeschool Music Curriculum: Ear, Keys, Theory

Choosing a homeschool music curriculum: ear-first music for ages 8 to 12, a first piano course, and music theory for teens, with free placement checks.

2026-11-26

Most homeschool music curriculum is one of two things: music appreciation, where a child listens to composers and learns their dates, or lessons on one instrument. Both have value. What often goes missing is the ear: hearing why music sounds the way it does, and making some of it. That is what lasts, whatever instrument comes later.

This page is for parents of children about 8 to 12, and of older beginners and teens, choosing a music plan. It covers what to look for and where our three guides fit.

Who this is for

  • Parents whose child asks to play something and has no lessons yet.
  • Families without a musician in the house.
  • Teens who want to understand the music they love, or start the piano.

What to look for in a homeschool music curriculum

Ear first. Beat, high and low, loud and soft, fast and slow come before notation.

Making, not only listening. A child should clap, sing and play something every session.

Composing early. Writing a short tune of their own teaches more than copying one.

Reading when it is useful. Notation makes sense once a child has something to write down.

A performance. Playing for the family at the end gives the weeks a goal.

Any instrument. A good starting plan works with what you have: a voice, hands to clap, a cheap keyboard or a free piano app. Buying an instrument can wait until your child knows they want to keep going.

Where our guides fit

All three are 12-week plans. Each is a PDF you run with any AI chat assistant, and the free versions of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot all work. Each week is one scripted session of about 60 minutes.

Make Music, for ages 8 to 12, moves from finding the beat through pitch, dynamics, tempo, happy and sad sounds, patterns and a first melody to layering a beat and a melody and writing an original tune, then a family demo day.

Your First Piano is for a first-time player at a keyboard. It covers the keyboard map and posture, a first melody, the left hand, hands together, reading music, chords, rhythm and dynamics, and ends with a real piece played for someone. A 61-key keyboard is enough.

Why Music Works is music theory for teens and adults: intervals, scales and keys, chords, progressions, rhythm, melody, tension and release, and why a song feels sad or triumphant, then writing and explaining a short piece.

The tutor rules stop the AI from composing or performing for the learner.

What they do not do

  • They do not replace a live teacher's ear on technique.
  • They do not include sheet music books or recordings.
  • They are not a music history or composer study course.

Questions parents ask

Do I need a musical background to teach music at home?

No. In our guides the AI tutor asks the questions and you listen. You need to be a good set of ears and to keep the session moving. Every session comes with the exact prompt to start it.

Is there a homeschool music curriculum for elementary school?

Make Music is written for ages 8 to 12. It starts with feeling a steady beat and ends with your child performing a tune they wrote. It works with any instrument, a keyboard app or just a voice.

What about middle school and high school music?

Your First Piano suits an older child or teen starting the keyboard, and Why Music Works teaches theory for teens and adults: intervals, scales, chords, progressions and how a song creates feeling.

Is there a free homeschool music curriculum?

Library recordings, free piano apps and online lessons cost nothing. A plan adds order. Each of our music guides has a free placement check that names the week to start at.
