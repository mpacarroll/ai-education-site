Good Manners, Real Confidence

Ages 7-11 · People and life

Not a nagging list of rules, but a done-for-you, 12-week plan for guiding a 7–11-year-old through the everyday manners that make them feel confident and welcome anywhere: a warm greeting, the magic words, the table, being a good guest, being a good host, practiced with an AI tutor that role-plays every scene so it sticks, instead of a rule your child hears once and forgets. You do not need to be Emily Post. You just need to sit beside them.

What you get

  • 12 deep weekly sessions, each a ready-to-run ~60-minute guide with a plain-language concept, a concrete worked example, a timed session plan with the AI-tutor prompts built in, a "show me" checkpoint (almost always a real moment with a real person), common mistakes to watch for, and optional practice.
  • A "Never used an AI chat assistant? Start here" onboarding that takes a complete beginner from zero to ready in about five minutes. No paid plan needed; any free AI assistant works.
  • A parent/teacher orientation: how to sit beside a learner using AI, what your job is and what the AI tutor's job is, and exactly what your one ~60-minute session a week looks like.
  • Four rescue prompts in the front matter for when the learner is embarrassed or stuck, bored, when the AI starts lecturing instead of coaching, and to check your child really understands.
  • This is a fully scripted, digital guide: every session is timed and every step is written out for you. No etiquette expertise needed.

The 12 weeks

  1. Why Manners Help You: a confidence skill, not a list of rules
  2. Greetings and Introductions: a confident hello
  3. The Magic Words: please, thank you, excuse me, sorry (and meaning it)
  4. Table Manners: The Basics
  5. Table Manners: Sharing the Table
  6. Being a Good Guest
  7. Being a Good Host
  8. Phone and Message Manners
  9. Writing a Thank-You Note
  10. Project Week I: Plan the Real Meal
  11. Project Week II: Do the Real Meal (for real)
  12. Family Demo Day: celebrate growth, choose what's next

Who it's for

Parents, teachers, and homeschoolers guiding a learner aged roughly 7–11 who's ready to handle themselves with real confidence: at the table, at a friend's house, on the phone, meeting someone new. No etiquette background needed, and no prior AI experience needed either. You lead one fully scripted ~60-minute session each week, every step timed and written out for you, while the AI tutor does the coaching and role-playing.

Why this instead of a free chatbot

A raw chatbot will happily recite a list of etiquette rules at your child, which teaches nothing that survives dinner. This pack ships the structure a chatbot lacks: a real 12-week arc built around the manners that actually make a kid feel welcome anywhere, a tutor that role-plays the moment (the new host, the phone call, the unfamiliar food at someone else's table) instead of just describing it, guardrails that keep every scene warm and pressure-free, and checkpoints that almost always send your child back out to try it with a real person. Curriculum + safe rehearsal + a way to see progress. That's the difference.

FAQ

  • Do I need to be an etiquette expert myself? No. The AI tutor coaches and role-plays every scene; you lead by showing up and asking for the checkpoint. Every session comes with the exact prompt to start it.
  • Which AI does it use? What if I've never used one? Works with any modern AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini), and the free version of any of them is enough; no paid plan needed. The guide's first section walks a complete beginner through setup in about five minutes, so you can start even if you've never opened an AI chat before.
  • Is my child's data collected? No. This is a digital guide sold to you, the adult. There's no login and no child account; you start and supervise every AI session yourself, under your own account. Your AI provider may store your chats, so the guide shows you what to keep out of them (a first name and age are all it needs).
  • What ages? Roughly 7–11.
  • We're not in the US. Will it fit? Yes. The guide is written in US English, and every launch prompt tells the AI to use your country or region (as you give it, as it can tell, or by asking you) for spelling, money, and examples. Manners vary by family too, and the guide tells you to let your own customs win.
