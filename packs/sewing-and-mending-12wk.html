Who it's for

Adults who want to stop throwing away clothes that could be fixed. Total beginners welcome. If you own a needle and thread (a machine is useful but not required until week 5), you're ready.

Why this instead of a free chatbot

A raw chatbot will give you a wall of generic sewing tips and no path from "can't thread a needle" to "can mend and make." This pack ships the structure and guardrails it lacks: a real 12-week progression from hand stitches to machine work to a finished project, and a tutor set up to ask what you're working on before it tells you what to do, and to stop you at the moments a needle or machine can actually hurt you. Real skill with a safety net, not a tip list.

Safety

This is educational content, not professional instruction. Needles, pins, scissors, irons, and sewing machines can injure: keep fingers clear of the needle, unplug the machine when threading it or clearing a jam, always cut away from yourself, and keep fingers clear of a hot iron and its steam. Work at a pace where you can stop the instant something feels wrong.

FAQ

  • Do I need any experience? None, with sewing or with AI. Week 1 is literally building your kit and learning the safety rules; every stitch is broken into small, checked steps.
  • I've never used an AI chat assistant. Is that a problem? No. The guide opens with a five-minute walkthrough that gets a complete beginner from zero to ready before Week 1 starts.
  • Which AI does it use? Any modern AI assistant: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini all work, and the free version of any of them is enough. Every step is written out for you; no special software and nothing to configure.
  • Is this safe? Who's liable? It's educational only. It teaches needle and machine safety from week one: fingers clear of the needle, unplug before threading or clearing a jam, cut away from yourself. You assume the usual risk of working with sharp tools; stop the moment something feels wrong.
  • Do I need a sewing machine? Not to start. Weeks 1 through 4 are entirely hand sewing. Weeks 5 and 6 teach the machine, so you'll need one for those (your own, a borrowed one, or one at a library or community sewing space), and a basic machine is all you need. Weeks 7 to 12 work by hand, with a machine as an option.
