You could piece this together for free from scattered forum threads and a chatbot that just guesses fixes with you, or you could get it as a real 12-week masterclass. This pack teaches the one mental model that lets you troubleshoot anything with a symptom: observe, isolate, hypothesize, test. An AI tutor walks beside you, asking what you actually see instead of naming a part to buy, pushing you to isolate before you guess, and making you state a testable hypothesis before you touch a tool. Master the loop on a toaster this month and you'll be running it on a router, a car, or a stuck process at work next year. The method transfers; a memorized fix list never does.

Format: A digital guide, self-paced over 12 weeks. Each week you lead one fully scripted ~60-minute session, with every step timed and written out for you. No expertise needed, no cohort, no deadlines.

Who it's for

Adults who throw things away or call for help the moment something breaks. No electronics or repair background assumed. If you can describe what you see and hear, you're ready.

Why this instead of a free chatbot

A raw chatbot will happily guess a part to replace before it even understands the symptom. This pack ships the structure and discipline it lacks: a tutor prompted to ask before it tells, a real 12-week progression from vague complaint to tested hypothesis, and a guardrail that will not let you skip straight to a guessed fix, or past a safety line into gas, mains electrical, or pressurized-system work that belongs to a licensed pro. A transferable mental model, not a longer list of fixes to memorize.

Safety

This is educational content, not professional advice. It builds confident diagnostic skill and, just as importantly, teaches you to recognize the jobs that are never yours: gas, mains or high-voltage electrical, and pressurized systems. Always unplug and power down before opening anything, and stop the moment you're unsure.
