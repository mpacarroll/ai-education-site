Learning to Read

Ages 5-8 · Reading and writing

Take the free placement check first: 15 minutes, and it names the week to start at.

Turn "will you read to me?" into "I can read it myself." This is a done-for-you, 12-week plan for guiding a 5–8-year-old from letter sounds all the way to reading a whole little book aloud to the family, using an AI tutor to generate endless patient practice while you do the leading. You do not need any special training to teach a child to read.

What you get

  • 12 deep weekly sessions, each about 60 minutes of practice split into short daily sittings of about ten minutes, and fully scripted: a plain-language concept explanation, a concrete worked example, a timed day-by-day session plan with the AI-tutor prompts built in, a "show me" checkpoint, common mistakes to watch for, and practice.
  • "Never used an AI chat assistant? Start here": a five-minute onboarding in the front matter that takes a complete beginner from zero to ready, using a free AI assistant. No paid plan needed.
  • Four rescue prompts in the front matter for when a session goes sideways (a stuck or frustrated child, boredom, the AI reading words for your child, or checking real understanding), so you're never stuck mid-session.
  • A digital guide you read on any device, no printing required.
  • Works wherever you live. The guide is written in US English, and every session tells the AI to use your country or region for spelling, letter names, and examples.

The 12 weeks

  1. Meeting the Alphabet: mapping which letter sounds are solid, shaky, or new
  2. Blending Sounds into Words: sliding sounds together, faster each round
  3. Word Detective: Sight Words (the first ten instant words)
  4. First Sentences: reading short sentences made of known words
  5. Cracking New Words: a repeatable sound-it-out strategy
  6. Little Stories Together: a tiny decodable story, read together
  7. Growing the Word Bank: more sight words, plus sh, ch, th
  8. Reading With Expression: matching your voice to the punctuation
  9. Building the Daily Habit: a ten-minute routine and a "just right" book
  10. Project Week I: Pick Your Book (a real reading plan)
  11. Project Week II: Read It (working through the book to the end)
  12. Story Time: Demo Day (read the whole book aloud, choose what's next)

Who it's for

Parents, teachers, and homeschoolers guiding a learner aged roughly 5–8 who is just starting to read or still building fluency. No teaching background needed and no prior AI experience needed; you lead about ten minutes of practice a day, most days of the week (roughly an hour a week), with every step timed and written out for you.

Why this instead of a free chatbot

A raw chatbot will happily read the word out loud the moment a child hesitates, which teaches nothing. This pack ships the structure a chatbot lacks: a real 12-week arc from letter sounds to a finished book, Socratic prompts that make the tutor wait for the child to try first, a hint-ladder guardrail that stops it handing over the word before your child has really tried, and checkpoints that prove the skill actually landed. Curriculum + guardrails + a bottomless supply of fresh, just-right practice. That's the difference.

FAQ

  • Do I need any training to teach reading? No. Every session is fully scripted (timed day-by-day plan, worked example, and the exact prompt to paste), so the AI tutor generates the practice and explains the sounds while you lead by asking for the "show me."
  • Which AI does it use? What if I've never used one? Any modern AI assistant works (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini), and the free version of each is enough; no paid plan needed. If you've never used an AI chat assistant before, the guide's first section walks you through setup in about five minutes.
  • Is my child's data collected? Not by the guide. It is a digital guide sold to you, the adult, with no login and no child account. You start and supervise every AI session yourself, under your own account. The AI provider may store those chats, so the guide asks you to share only a first name and age, nothing private.
  • What ages? Roughly 5–8, from first letter sounds through early fluency.
