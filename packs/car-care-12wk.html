Why this instead of a free chatbot

A raw chatbot will happily talk you into getting under a car on a wobbly jack. This pack ships the structure and guardrails it lacks: a diagnose-first method, a real 12-week progression, and a tutor set up to confirm the engine is cool and the car is on jack stands before you touch anything — and to stop you and route you to a certified mechanic when a job is genuinely dangerous (brakes, airbags, steering, timing belts). Competence with a safety net, not confident guessing.

Safety

This is educational content, not professional advice. It teaches confident-DIY-level maintenance — fluids, tires, battery, bulbs, filters, reading a code — and, just as importantly, how to recognize the jobs that need a certified mechanic: brakes, airbags, steering and suspension, timing belts. Only lift a car on rated jack stands on level ground, let the engine cool before you open the cooling system, respect the battery, and stop when unsure.

FAQ

  • Do I need any experience? None. Week 1 is just reading your own dashboard and manual; every task is broken into small, checked steps.
  • Which AI does it use? Any modern AI chat assistant — ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini all work, and a free version is enough; no paid plan needed. Never used one before? The guide's first section walks complete beginners through setup in about five minutes before Week 1 begins.
  • Is this safe? Who's liable? It's educational only and teaches you to stop at dangerous work and see a certified mechanic (brakes, airbags, steering/suspension, timing belts). You assume the usual DIY risk — only work on a cool engine, only go under a car on rated jack stands, respect the battery and coolant, and don't improvise past your comfort.
  • Will this really stop me getting overcharged? Week 9 is a whole week on reading a repair quote — parts vs labor vs fees, "required" vs "recommended" — so you can question a bill politely and know when to get a second opinion.
  • Do I need tools or a garage? Barely. Most weeks need only a rag, a tire gauge, and a cheap OBD-II reader; a driveway is plenty.
