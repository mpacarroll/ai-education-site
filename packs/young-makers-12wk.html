Turn "I have an idea" into something real. This is a done-for-you, 12-week plan for guiding a 10–14-year-old from noticing an everyday problem to sketching it, prototyping it out of whatever's in the junk drawer, testing it on a real person, and showing off an invention they built themselves, using an AI tutor to ask the questions while you do the leading. You do not need to be inventive, handy, or technical yourself.

What you get

  • 12 deep weekly sessions, each fully scripted: a plain-language concept explanation, a concrete worked example, a minute-by-minute session plan with the AI-tutor prompts built in, a "show me" checkpoint so you always know it landed, common mistakes to watch for, and practice to reinforce the idea. You don't need to be inventive, handy, or technical; every step is written out for you.
  • A "Never used an AI chat assistant? Start here" onboarding that takes about five minutes: picking a free AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini all work), setting up an account, and getting comfortable pasting a prompt, with no paid plan and no prior AI experience needed.
  • A parent/teacher orientation: how to sit beside a learner using AI, why the adult stays the leader and the AI is the tutor, and exactly what your one ~60-minute session a week looks like.
  • Four rescue prompts in the front matter: for when the learner is frustrated or stuck, for when they're bored, for when the AI starts designing or building the invention FOR the learner, and for checking they really understand.
  • A Socratic tutor built into every session plan: the tutor asks before it tells, and a guardrail keeps the AI from ever designing or building the invention FOR the learner.
  • Safety built in: adult supervision for anything sharp, hot, or electrical, and prompts that have the AI say "check with your adult first" before any blade, hot glue, needle, or battery.
  • Works wherever you live: the guide is written in US English, and every prompt tells the AI to use your country or region for spelling, units, money, school terms, and examples.

The 12 weeks

  1. Meet the Maker Mindset: notice, imagine, build, test, improve
  2. Problems Worth Solving: turn an annoyance into a real problem
  3. Who Is It For? A first real interview
  4. A Hundred Ideas: brainstorming without judging
  5. Sketch & Plan It: pick an idea and plan it on paper
  6. Build a Rough Prototype: using whatever's on hand
  7. Test It on a Real Person: show, don't tell
  8. Make It Better: iterate from real feedback
  9. Tell Its Story: explain it in under a minute
  10. Invention Project Week I: Plan It (a real invention, scoped and signed off)
  11. Invention Project Week II: Build It
  12. Showcase Your Invention: family demo day, choose what's next

Who it's for

Parents, teachers, and homeschoolers guiding a learner aged roughly 10–14 who likes making things: with their hands, with words, with whatever's in the junk drawer. No inventing, engineering, or design background needed, and no prior AI experience needed. You lead one fully scripted ~60-minute session each week, with every step timed and written out for you.

Why this instead of a free chatbot

A raw chatbot will happily hand your kid a finished idea or a fully designed solution the moment they ask, which skips the entire skill this pack teaches. This pack ships the structure a chatbot lacks: a real 12-week arc that runs the actual maker loop (notice, imagine, build, test, improve), Socratic prompts that make the tutor ask before it tells, a guardrail that stops it from ever designing or building the invention for the learner, and checkpoints that prove the child can do it themselves. Curriculum + guardrails + a way to see progress. That's the difference.

FAQ

  • Do I need to be inventive or handy myself? No. The AI tutor does the questioning and coaching; you lead by staying present and doing the "show me" checkpoint. Every session comes with the exact prompt to start it.
  • Which AI does it use? What if I've never used one? It works with any modern AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini), and the free version of any of them is enough; no paid plan required. The guide's first section walks complete beginners through setup in about five minutes, so it's fine if you've never used an AI chat assistant before.
  • Is my child's data collected? Not by us. This is a digital guide sold to you, the adult. The guide needs no login and there's no child account; you start and supervise every AI session yourself, under your own account. AI providers may store chats, so the guide has you share only a first name and age.
  • Does this cost money to run, or involve tools or selling? No. Prototypes are built from everyday household materials (cardboard, tape, paper, recycling-bin finds), with nothing to buy and no special tools required. Anything sharp or hot (scissors, a glue gun, a needle) is done with an adult supervising. The whole pack is about making, problem-solving, and creativity; there's no selling, pricing, or finance content anywhere in it.
  • What ages? Roughly 10–14.
